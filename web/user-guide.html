PGP for Outlook — User's Guide

Everything you need to send and receive encrypted email, without needing to know how PGP works under the hood.

What this add-in does

It encrypts the body of your email messages (and, on supported versions of Outlook, attachments too) so that only your intended recipients — the people who also have this add-in and a PGP key — can read them. Anyone intercepting the message in transit, or with access to the mail server, sees only unreadable scrambled text.

Subject lines and the To/From/Cc headers are not encrypted — this is a limitation of email itself, not this add-in. Don't put anything sensitive in the subject line.

One-time setup: generate your key

  1. Open Manage PGP from the Outlook ribbon.
  2. Click Generate New Key Pair, enter your name and confirm your email address, and choose a strong passphrase. Write this passphrase down somewhere safe — if you lose it, no one (including your IT department) can recover your encrypted mail.
  3. That's it. Your key is generated and stored securely, synced automatically to every device you use Outlook on.

Sending an encrypted message

  1. Compose a new message as normal, and address it to your recipient(s).
  2. Open the Encrypt pane from the ribbon.
  3. The pane looks up a PGP key for each recipient automatically. If a recipient doesn't have one on file yet, you'll see that here — you can't encrypt to someone without a key.
  4. Click Encrypt Message. Your message body is replaced with encrypted text.
  5. Click Send in Outlook as normal.
Changed your mind after encrypting? A Decrypt Message button appears in its place — click it to restore the original message and start over.

Reading an encrypted message

Open the message and select the Decrypt pane from the ribbon. Enter your passphrase once per session (it's remembered for 15 minutes of inactivity) and the original message appears.

Automatic encryption and sending (optional, off by default)

In Manage PGP → Personal Preferences, two optional settings can make sending encrypted mail faster if you send it often to the same people:

Automatically encrypt if all recipients have valid keys

When this is on, opening the Encrypt pane on a new message automatically encrypts it the moment every recipient's key has been found — you don't need to click Encrypt yourself. If any recipient doesn't have a key, or the message needs your attention for another reason (like an embedded image that can't be encrypted), it's left for you to handle manually, exactly as it works today.

Automatically send once auto-encryption completes

This setting sends your message with no further review. Once encryption succeeds, the message is sent immediately — there is no confirmation step, and once it starts, there is no way to stop it. Only turn this on if you're confident your recipients and message content are correct before you finish composing. This option is only available when "Automatically encrypt" (above) is also on, and only on newer versions of Outlook that support it.

Frequently asked questions

What if I lose my passphrase?

There is no way to recover an encrypted message without it — not even your IT administrator can help. Store it somewhere safe (a password manager is ideal).

Can I read my own sent messages later?

Yes. Every message you encrypt is automatically also encrypted to your own key, so you can always decrypt and re-read anything you've sent.

What happens if I send an attachment?

On Outlook 2021, Microsoft 365, and Outlook on the web, attachments are encrypted individually alongside the message body. On older versions of Outlook, attachment encryption isn't available — you'll be asked to confirm removing attachments before the message can be encrypted.

Questions not answered here? Contact your IT administrator or the team that manages this add-in for your organization.
